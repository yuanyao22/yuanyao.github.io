## Abstract

*Summary of the [published commentary](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41370-026-00996-y), which has no separate abstract.*

This commentary examines how community collaboration can strengthen air toxics research near industrial facilities. Drawing on a Philadelphia VOC monitoring study, it discusses community-informed sampling and challenges in identifying pollution sources. It recommends more detailed monitoring, broader exposure assessment, and sustained resident participation to improve the relevance and communication of research.

## Key Findings

- **Community-informed monitoring:** In the Philadelphia study discussed, residents helped select sampling sites and received interim results; year-long passive sampling detected 38 VOCs.
- **Source attribution:** Similar vehicle and refinery emission profiles complicate source separation. The commentary recommends higher-frequency measurements and calibrated sensors.
- **Exposure and risk assessment:** Recommendations include paired indoor, outdoor, and personal monitoring, time-activity information, and toxicity-informed assessment of VOC mixtures.
- **Sustained participation:** Residents can contribute to deployment, quality assurance, interpretation, and communication. Accessible reporting can support informed exposure-reduction decisions.
